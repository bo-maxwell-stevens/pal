# FRED-AMF Figure Trends Summary

## Scope

This summary interprets current figures `Fig1` to `Fig8` generated by the full FRED4-primary pipeline.

## Main trends

- Full FRED4 is clearly the best trait source for overlap with both AMF datasets.
- GlobalAMFungi shows stronger species overlap than EcoBank under strict canonical matching.
- Trait coverage varies across species in the multivariate species-level fits.
- Genus-level sensitivity analyses provide larger effective sample sizes and are useful as a stability check.

## Figure-by-figure interpretation

### Fig1: Dataset overlap summary

- Strongest signal in the full analysis.
- Exact species overlap:
  - GlobalAMFungi x FRED4_full: 121 / 275 (44.0%)
  - EcoBank x FRED4_full: 160 / 500 (32.0%)
- Filtered FRED4 files and FRED3 have lower overlap and should remain sensitivity sources.

### Fig2: Geographic coverage

- FRED4 has broad global coverage.
- EcoBank and GlobalAMFungi are also geographically broad but reflect different sampling structure.
- AMF observations and trait measurements are integrated by taxon and are not paired by sample location/time.

### Fig3: Root trait distributions

- Overlap subsets (EcoBank/GlobalAMFungi intersecting FRED4 species) are narrower than all-FRED distributions.
- Root P and mycorrhizal colonization coverage remain sparse relative to diameter/SRL/RTD.

### Fig4: Root economics PCA

- PCA is interpretable with trait loading arrows and percent variance labels.
- Woodiness and dataset membership show broad structure, but not complete separation.
- PC-richness panels summarize gradients across the integrated datasets.

### Fig5: Species-level AMF richness vs traits

- Trends are reported from complete-case subsets for multi-trait overlap.

### Fig6: AMF guild proportions vs traits

- Guild metrics are now correctly transformed from percent scale to unit proportion before logit.
- Guild metrics are reported after percent-to-proportion conversion and logit transformation.

### Fig7: Woody vs herbaceous sensitivity

- Woodiness grouping has been corrected so non-woody taxa are no longer misclassified.
- Paired panels are now interpretable as a sensitivity check, with expected subgroup-n constraints.

### Fig8: Genus-level sensitivity

- Helpful as a robustness layer because genus-level aggregation increases overlap.
- Point size and color both represent species support per genus; large/high-support genera carry more weight visually.
- Example: `carex` contributes very high species support in this analysis.

## Interesting points worth follow-up

- Full FRED4 gives a meaningful overlap increase over filtered sources.
- GlobalAMFungi appears to align better with FRED4 trait coverage than EcoBank at species level.
- Several high-AMF-richness taxa have sparse trait coverage, which may bias trait-richness slope stability.

## Data and model notes

- Trait and AMF values are integrated at taxon level and are not necessarily from the same place/time.
- Root N and Root P are environmentally plastic and may dilute species-mean trait signals.
- Root traits are phylogenetically structured; non-phylogenetic OLS can mix lineage and ecology effects.
- Fuzzy name matches are not automatically accepted.
